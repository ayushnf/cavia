A private learning institution for familiesBy invitation · ages four to twelve

A child is not a curriculum waiting to happen.

Cavia is a small learning community for families. It starts with one child: what they can already do, what they care about, what they need next. Then it builds the education around that, using family, places, people and technology where they help.

01Why

Most education starts with the material. The child is asked to fit.

A syllabus is written years before it meets a particular child. It cannot know that she reads three years ahead and still counts on her fingers, or that he will concentrate for an hour on anything that involves the lake. So the system is organised around what can be scheduled, and the child adapts to it. Or doesn't.

Most of what is sold as personalisation keeps this order and changes the surface: the same content, served faster, with more colours.

We think the order should be reversed. Understand the learner first. Then decide what to teach, where, with whom, and how you will know it worked.

The usual starting point
  1. aWhat lesson should the child do today?
  2. bWhich resource should we use?
  3. cIs the child on grade level?
Cavia's starting point
  1. aWhat should move forward for this child, now?
  2. bWhat experience, place or person would make that worth doing?
  3. cWhich capabilities are secure, developing, or not yet known?
02A different model

Not less rigour. A different route to it.

Cavia keeps a stable spine: reading and writing, mathematics, science, thinking, making, the body, other people. Every child covers all of it, and it is checked for gaps.

What changes is the route. Goals are chosen for this child now. The context is chosen before the material. The plan is revised when evidence arrives, not when the calendar says so. A week has a rhythm rather than a timetable.

Same destination. The road is drawn around the child, and redrawn as the child changes.

Forty identical periods. The same sequence for every child.

Commitment one

Meaning before material

Start with a real question, need or context. Choose content because it helps the child do something that matters to them.

Commitment two

Evidence before assumption

Use the child's work, what adults observe, and what the child says. No fixed learning styles, no personality types, no labels used to predict who a child is.

Commitment three

Change the environment before labelling the child

When progress stalls, alter the task, the scaffold, the setting or the support first. Trait explanations come last, if at all.

Commitment four

Care before automation

Technology holds complexity. It does not replace judgement, a relationship, or a good day outside.

03The experience

What a week looks like.

Cavia is not a screen a child sits in front of. A week starts from something the child actually asked or wanted, and is planned backwards from there: where to go, who to ask, what has to be taught directly, what needs practice, what the child will make, and how we will know it worked.

    04The system

    How Cavia understands a learner, and adapts.

    Underneath every week is one loop. It runs for every goal, from an afternoon to a year, and the family can see each turn of it. Follow it around.

    The loop, not the lesson, is the product.

    Station 1 · Learner

    What is known, uncertain and changing about this child.

    Not a profile. A living record of knowledge, skills, strategies, motivation, agency and wellbeing, each with its evidence and its confidence attached.

    Station 2 · Context

    The family's values, routines, places and people.

    Where the child lives, what the family cares about, who is nearby, what a workable week looks like. Context is modelled, not assumed.

    Station 3 · Goal

    One thing that should move forward now.

    Chosen against a stable map of capabilities so that following curiosity never quietly opens a gap.

    Station 4 · Pathway

    A route through experiences, instruction and practice.

    The lake, the map, twelve minutes of direct teaching, ten minutes of practice. Sequenced for this child, with the expected evidence defined before starting.

    Station 5 · Experience

    The child makes, explains, solves or performs something.

    Projects, books, walks, mentors, workshops, games, conversations. The graph of what is available is chosen from, not marched through.

    Station 6 · Evidence

    What actually changed, and how sure we are.

    Work products, observations, the child's own reflection, a check after time has passed. Direct evidence outweighs inference. Every claim keeps its provenance.

    Station 7 · Adaptation

    The record updates. The next goal is chosen.

    Reflection with the child and the parent, then the loop turns again. The adult decides; the system proposes and shows its reasons.

    Plate VThe learner state is drawn as terrain because that is how it behaves: it rises where evidence accumulates, it shifts, and there are always regions nobody has surveyed yet. Move the week to watch one child's map change. Hatching is honesty, not decoration.

    Underneath all of it is an AI layer. Two kinds of model do most of the work: sensing models that turn what a child makes, says and does into evidence, and generative models that draft the next pathway, task or material for that child. Neither is the authority. Both show their working, and both stop at the bedrock, where an adult decides.

    The models are advanced. The claims are modest. That is the correct way round.

    Where all the evidence comes from.

    A walk, a map, a page of writing, a buckle. Life is the surface layer and the only one the child needs to see.

    • Projects, books, walks, mentors, conversations
    • Direct teaching and practice, in small doses

    Not a profile

    A state, with its uncertainty showing

    The record is dynamic and evidence based. It never hardens into a type or a label, and it says how much it does not know.

    Provenance

    Every claim keeps its evidence

    What was observed, when, by whom, under what conditions, and how confident the system is. Inferences are marked as inferences.

    Authority

    The adult decides

    AI here is infrastructure, not authority. It organises signals, proposes options and shows its reasons. It cannot know a child on its own, and it is never allowed to pretend otherwise.

    05The research

    We show our working.

    Cavia is being shaped by people working across several fields at once, and we keep three kinds of statement apart: what research already tells us, what Cavia believes, and what Cavia is still exploring. Families deserve to know which is which.

    The standard is not certainty. It is honesty about what warrants confidence.

    Known

    Learning depends on more than the material in front of a child.

    Prior knowledge, motivation, culture, relationships and the setting all shape what is learned. No single framework captures every learner.

    Known

    Practice spaced over time, retrieval, feedback and reflection support durable learning.

    Useful principles, not a template. They work with a child's world, not instead of it.

    Known

    Autonomy, competence and relatedness matter for motivation.

    Children persist at things they have some say over, can get better at, and share with people they trust.

    Cavia believes

    A living learner record can be more accountable than a static profile.

    A record that keeps evidence, uncertainty and context visible should make better decisions possible. This is a design choice we are testing, not a result.

    Cavia believes

    Context can create a better reason to learn without lowering the bar.

    The lake does not replace the arithmetic. It makes the arithmetic worth doing, and we still teach and practise it directly.

    Exploring

    What evidence shows growth without making childhood feel like constant measurement?

    We want forms of evidence that are useful to the child and the family, not only to a system.

    Exploring

    When should a digital system make room for a person or a place instead?

    Sometimes the right recommendation is to close the device and go outside. We are studying when, and whether families experience that as useful.

    Cavia is invite only because learning is a relationship, not an audience.

    The circle stays small enough for families, mentors and educators to know one another, and for what one family notices to be useful to another. A grandparent, a neighbour who repairs things, a scientist who will answer a nine-year-old's email, the lake nearby. They enter a child's learning environment because someone in the circle brought them in.

    Who we are looking for

    Families who want to make considered choices rather than collect resources. People willing to say what they notice, and to let it change the plan. Adults with something real to teach, who would enjoy being asked by a child.

    07The invitation

    Tell us about your child.

    Not a test score. Not a grade. What have you noticed lately? What are they drawn to, what do they avoid, what would you like their education to make possible?

    We read every request as the beginning of a conversation. Cavia is not for everyone, and it should be right for someone: there is no promise of admission, and no assumption that one kind of education fits every family.

    This is an expression of interest, not an assessment, a diagnosis or a contract. This page stores nothing; what you write here is shown back to you and goes no further until a member of the team writes to you.

    RResearch

    Careful ideas deserve careful testing.

    This page is the register we keep for ourselves. It separates what research has already established, what Cavia believes and is building on, and what we are still exploring and intend to test. When the register changes, this page changes.

    Established

    Findings with broad support across learning and developmental science. We use them as constraints on the design, not as marketing.

    Known

    Learning is a change in the learner produced by interaction with tasks, people, tools and environments over time.

    So a learning system has to model both the learner and the context the learning happens in. Neither alone is enough.

    Known

    Prior knowledge, memory, retrieval, spaced practice, feedback and transfer shape what stays learned.

    A personalised education still needs systematic knowledge and vocabulary, and still needs to be checked after time has passed.

    Known

    Autonomy, competence and relatedness sustain motivation.

    Choice should expand as capability and self-regulation grow, not be handed over all at once.

    Known

    Guided play and experiential learning support development when an adult shapes the activity.

    Meaningful activity, exploration and active participation, with adult guidance, rather than either free play or a worksheet.

    Known

    Metacognition, planning, monitoring and reflection can be taught, at an age-appropriate scale.

    Children can be asked to plan, monitor and review. The activities must fit the age.

    Known

    Fixed learning styles and personality types do not predict how a child should be taught.

    Cavia does not use them. Learner characteristics inform adaptation only when the evidence is useful and the characteristic is relevant to the task.

    What Cavia believes

    Design positions we have taken deliberately. Each is a hypothesis with a reason, and each can be wrong.

    Belief

    The unit of design should be the goal and the learner's current state, not the lesson or the resource.

    The whitepaper calls this the canonical cycle: purpose, context, goal, experience, instruction and practice, performance, reflection, evidence, update, next goal.

    Belief

    A stable spine with a flexible surface resolves the rigid-timetable versus unstructured-learning argument.

    Capability families and broad progression are stable across years. Pace, emphasis, context and route adapt at the level of the term, the week, the day and the task.

    Belief

    A learner record that carries evidence, provenance and confidence is more accountable than a profile.

    Direct performance outweighs inference. Independent evidence outweighs repeated observation from one source. Inferences are marked as inferences.

    Belief

    AI should be infrastructure, never the educational authority.

    A parent assistant must not overstate certainty or turn observations into diagnoses. A child assistant must promote human relationships and independence. High-stakes decisions belong to people.

    Belief

    Change the environment before labelling the child.

    If progress stalls, modify difficulty, scaffolding, setting or support before reaching for a trait-level explanation.

    What we are exploring

    Research questions, in the order we expect to work on them. Major causal claims will be preregistered and independently reviewed before we make them.

    Begin with a design cohort of thirty to fifty families. Iterate the parent and learner models. Then a larger pilot with outcomes specified in advance. Only then, controlled studies.

    1. Does contextual pathway design improve durable learning compared with resource-first tutoring, when foundational practice is held constant?
    2. Does parent capability training reduce parent workload per unit of learner progress?
    3. Can the learner model predict useful next steps without harmful over-inference?
    4. Does meaningful agency improve persistence and transfer without reducing foundational coverage?
    5. What combination of family, peer and mentor interaction is sufficient for social development in mainly home-based settings?
    6. Which evidence types most reliably support school transition and later credentialing?
    7. Can strong child–AI safeguards be implemented without materially reducing educational usefulness?

    Safeguarding and data

    • Collect only data that serves a clear educational purpose.
    • Parents control collection, visibility, export and deletion where applicable.
    • Child profiles are never sold or used for advertising.
    • Sensitive inferences are kept apart from ordinary records and never presented as facts without evidence.
    • Human review for high-impact decisions.
    • Optimise for learning and wellbeing, not time spent with the AI.

    Credentials and school transition

    Home-based learning does not automatically produce a school-equivalent credential, and we do not claim that it does. Cavia keeps routes open: a record that maps a child's evidence to the requirements of an external pathway, and a transition plan for families who move to or from a mainstream school. In India, NIOS is one such route; Cambridge and Pearson have private-candidate routes subject to their current rules. Eligibility changes and is checked case by case.

    AFor autistic learners

    Learning should make room for the whole child.

    Cavia for autistic learners is a research-led initiative for families who want an education shaped around a child's strengths, communication, interests, routines, sensory context and changing development. It is free for parents of autistic children.

    Free for families of autistic children

    No autism story is a template for another child.

    This work begins with listening. It asks what helps this child feel understood, available for learning and able to take part. It does not reduce a child to a diagnosis, a score, or a list of deficits, and it does not assume a sensory or communication profile before meeting the child.

    The conditions around learning are part of learning.

    We pay attention to

    Communication

    How a child understands, expresses preferences, and is best met in conversation.

    We pay attention to

    Sensory context

    What in an environment supports participation and what makes it harder. Personal, not presumed.

    We pay attention to

    Interests and strengths

    What a child notices deeply, returns to, and may want to use as a way into new learning.

    We pay attention to

    Rhythm and relationship

    Routines, transitions, recovery, trusted people, and the practical shape of a family's days.

    A different kind of plan

    Listen

    Begin with the child and family

    Build a shared picture of strengths, needs, hopes, and what makes a day workable.

    Adjust

    Design the environment with care

    Communication, sensory context, pace, transitions and available support, considered before content.

    Explore

    Make a path with meaning

    Connect the child's interests to worthwhile learning experiences and clear goals.

    Reflect

    Keep learning from the child

    Notice what supported participation, revise what did not, and make the next step together.

    Support for learning. Not a substitute for care.

    Cavia does not diagnose autism, provide therapy, make clinical recommendations, or replace a child's existing specialists.

    1. iWe do not assume a sensory or communication profile.
    2. iiWe do not treat difference as a problem to correct.
    3. iiiWe keep decisions visible to families and invite challenge.
    4. ivWe take guidance from autistic people, families and specialists seriously.

    What informs this work

    Known

    Autism is diverse. Needs and abilities vary, and can change over time.

    A learning plan has to be built with the child and family rather than imported from a type.

    Known

    Physical and social environments affect participation.

    Guidance for children and young people includes attending to individual sensory sensitivities and making appropriate adjustments.

    Belief

    A learner record that keeps context visible may help families make better educational choices.

    A design hypothesis that needs ongoing critique and research with autistic people, families and specialists.

    Exploring

    What makes a digital learning system feel useful, safe and respectful to autistic children and their families?

    To be determined with the community, not assumed by a product team.
